Forest of Senses

An audio-visual environment that induces measurable motor adaptation without any explicit instruction, driven by a 5-finger isometric force sensor.

Context

Conventional interactive rehabilitation relies on explicit instructions: "press this finger, reach this target, hold N seconds". That ensures task comprehension but stifles spontaneous exploration and the intrinsic motivation that sustains a full session. Forest of Senses starts from the opposite hypothesis: motor adaptation can be induced without any instruction.

The environment targets two axes relevant to post-stroke rehab: finger individuation (suppressing synkinesis) and sub-maximal force regulation (aiming at an invisible force target).

Approach

The five fingers' isometric forces are captured by the Dextrain Manipulandum (~14 Hz) and fed into a Unity environment. A four-phase state machine progressively alters the contingency between effort and feedback (winner-takes-all rule, virtual fatigue gauge, invisible sub-maximal target) without ever revealing them.

No interface, no score, no timer, no text: the scenery signals progress. The winter-to-spring transition mirrors the non-linear return of motor function typical of post-stroke recovery. Each frame is logged to CSV and analyzed offline in Python (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Cohen's d).

"With this sensor, you control what happens in the world. Explore freely. There are 4 phases; the first is just a one-minute calibration phase." — the only instruction given.

Results

Pilot study on 15 healthy subjects (21–63 years):

  • Net isolation force: 2.88 N → 4.53 N (+57%), p = 0.0020, d = 0.99.
  • Sub-maximal accuracy: 43.4% → 60.4% (+39%), p = 0.0054, d = 0.86.
  • 86.7% of subjects (13/15) consciously identified the isolation rule; auditory modality dominates visual.
